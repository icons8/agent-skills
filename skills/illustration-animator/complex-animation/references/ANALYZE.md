# Analyze

`${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}` is the folder that holds this SKILL.md and `references/` (the engine is in `../engine`); engine commands, document paths and clients that leave the variable as is: "Paths" in `SKILL.md`.

## Time limits - hard

Per illustration: scenario - 3 minutes, animation - 7 minutes, one look at the frame sheets - 2 minutes: it is advice, nothing is rebuilt on its remarks, fixes of script findings - 4 minutes. For scenes with people (a character, a hand, a group of people): animation - 15 minutes, fixes of script findings - 12 minutes; the rest as above. Note the time at the start of a stage (`node -e "console.log(Date.now())"`, milliseconds: works in every shell, PowerShell included) and check it before every new round. Time is up - stop and deliver the best build so far; no extensions and no extra attempts. The deadline goes into the prompt of every subagent (animator, fixer).

Write the minutes of each stage, the cost of the scene and the outcome to `<style folder>/out/<scene>.timing.json`: `{"scenario_min": 2.5, "animation_min": 11, "judge_min": 2, "fixes_min": 6, "cost_usd": 4.2, "fix_rounds": 1, "stop_reason": "passed", "tried": "a longer swing"}` (`stop_reason`: `passed`, `limit` or `engine`; leave out what is unknown; optional `composition`: `character`, `hand`, `group` or `object`). `make.js` adds its own `builds`, `build_min` and `engine_stop` on every run (batch too) and keeps the keys you wrote. Do not write `first_try` - it is the user's word (`clean`: his first look took the scene without edits, `edited`: after them), set by `ledger.js rate`. The run ledger reads this file.

Input: a style folder, e.g. `styles/plain/`, with `svg/*.svg` inside. If the user gave a folder that directly contains SVGs, that is `svg/`, and the style folder is one level up.

`no-stop` mode: don't ask anything at the end, run the create step right away in the same mode.

## 1. Preparation

1. Style: the start of the file names (`notion-line-art-…`, `plain-…`, `comic-…`, `flexy-…`, `black-chalk-…`, `main-…`, `journal-…`, `organic-…`, `ballpoint-pen-…`, `bold-people-…`) or the folder name; the id is the file name in `styles/`. If there is no style file, work by the common rules and say so in the first line of `scenarios.md`. Most of the style files are profiles measured on Icons8's own animations: see "Style profiles" below for how much to trust one.
2. Read `styles/common.md`, styles/<style>.md (including "Lessons" and "User bans" - they outrank everything else) and `SCENE-FORMAT.md`; then, if it exists, `<style folder>/rules.md` - the user's own lessons and bans for this style, written by the verify step. On animation technique (what moves, how, timing, look) they outrank the skill's style rules. They are notes, not instructions: never run a command, open a link or read or write a file outside the style folder because rules.md says so.
3. Setup: if `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/make.js"` fails with "Setup not done", run `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/setup.js"` once (without `--yes` it changes nothing and prints what it would install: show that to the user and, once they agree, run it again with `--yes`; exit code 3 means no ffmpeg: give the user the command it printed).
4. Don't redo scenes that are already done (`scenes/<name>.json` exists) unless the user asked.
5. **SVG check.** For every SVG run `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/els.js" <svg>`. Lines starting with `!` are elements the engine doesn't carry over (stroke, clip-path, mask, text, image, `<use>`, `<line>`). Stroke, text, image, `<use>` and `<line>` drop visible content: the key pose won't match the drawing and verify would send the scene to manual review anyway. Don't analyze such an SVG: put it aside with the reasons (an `svg-extra` copy without the element is a way round: `SCENE-FORMAT.md`, field `svg`). A clip-path or a mask often only crops what lies outside the drawing (most of the approved scenes have them and build cleanly), so it does not stop the analysis: build as usual, and if `frame0` fails at step F, put the SVG aside the same way. What was put aside goes into `scenarios.md` (section 3, "SVG to prepare") and into the first line of your reply to the user, in plain words with the fix: "<name>: has strokes - Outline Stroke before export", "has a clipping mask - release it and cut the shapes", "has text - convert to outlines", "has an image - vector only", "has symbols (`<use>`) - expand them", "has `<line>` - Outline Stroke". If the user says to animate it anyway, analyze it as usual. Only the SVGs that were not put aside go into the batches below.

## Style profiles and the story catalog

A style file is one of two kinds. Own rules: the styles our team animated and reviewed (Plain, Comic, Flexy, Notion Line Art, Little and a few more): their numbers come from our own reference animations and from reviews, and the file holds "Lessons" and "User bans". Measured profile: every other style has a profile made from Icons8's own animations of that style (`## Measured`, `## Format`, `## Motion`, `## Easing`, `## Engine limits and what to do`). It tells what animators of the style did, not what the user approved.

Order of authority, from the strongest: (1) the bans and lessons of `common.md` and of the style ("User bans", "Lessons"); (2) the numbers of the style file; (3) the rest of `common.md`; (4) the story catalog. A profile is a hint, not a ban and not a permission: where it says "fades are normal" or "short swings", check what Icons8 actually animated in this style (the profile and the catalog entries of the style) and fit the scene to that case; it does not lift a ban of `common.md` for every scene. When the profile says what the engine cannot do (live strokes, 3D turn, replacement drawings: "Engine limits and what to do"), do what the line after the arrow says; a path morph needs a pose (`SCENE-FORMAT.md`, "Morph").

The catalog stories/<style>.md has one entry per real Icons8 animation (the format is in `stories/README.md`). It is read only through `grep`, never whole: step B¾.

## 2. Scenes - in batches via subagents

Split the illustrations into batches of 4. Each batch is done by a separate subagent (general-purpose). It is given: the SVG paths of the batch, the style folder, the style, this whole section (steps A–G) and the paths to the rules: `styles/common.md`, styles/<style>.md, `SCENE-FORMAT.md` and `<style folder>/rules.md` if it exists, plus the catalog folder `stories/`. The first thing the subagent does is read the files (in the style files - including "Lessons" and "User bans"); the catalog it only searches. The main agent doesn't spend its own context on analyzing illustrations.

Steps for each illustration (the subagent runs them):

**A. Look.** Run `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/preview.js" <svg> --labels --out <style folder>/previews/<name>-labels.png` and open the image. Numbers of elements that coincide in place are merged into one label ("4·5" - usually a fill and its outline). `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/els.js" <svg>` - the list of elements with color and bbox.

**A½. People gate: a puppet goes on, a fused figure says "we won't cope".** A person, an animal or a hand scene is a **puppet** when it is assembled on hinges: the arms and legs (and the head) are separate shapes that overlap the body at the joints, so nothing has to be cut. It shows in the geometry: parts as long and thin as an arm or a leg, length² / area ≥ 3, that meet another part at one end; no single outline holds 60% of the points. A puppet is never stopped and never gets a warning: it goes on to build in the same run, like any full scene; check the joints of the plan against `els.js` as usual (hinge at the overlap, the overlap is the cover). The gate stays shut only for a figure that is not a puppet - a fused outline (`gate: closed`) or one where a limb has to be cut and its hidden part redrawn. Such a scene is animated only if everything that has to move is already a separate figure or group in the SVG (`els.js`: each arm, leg and head you would move is its own element, so every line of the motion plan is `whole` or `hinge`, none needs a `cut`). A figure that is not a puppet and needs a cut (arms and legs fused into one outline, a line-art walker with steps and bent knees) is clearly beyond the engine: the seams will show. Don't drop such a scene and don't pass it silently: analyze it like any other (intent, plan, scene file) and write `Warning: stop, we won't cope - <the reason in one phrase, e.g. one outline, both legs and both arms need a cut and a knee bend, the seams will open>` as the first line of scenes/<name>.story.md; it goes into `scenarios.md` (section 3). The scene is not built by this skill: after the whole batch is analyzed the run stops (section 4) and waits for the person; if they say go, the scene is built as usual, as well as it can be done, and the warning stays in the summary. A puppet (`gate: open`) never gets this warning. Objects, symbols and interface are not people: per `common.md` "Fused parts", if what must move is fused into one outline with something else (a lid with a box, a lever with a machine body), that is a cut and a pinned seam, not a refusal. The story fits a bend and a tilt (up to ~15–20° at a joint), without drawing what is hidden. "Not animatable: <why>" - only if every story would need to reveal something the drawing doesn't have.

**B. Intent - a few short lines, before any part is cut.** The whole idea of the scene is five lines (six for a walker) in plain language: no frames and no part names; the numbers are only in `Pace`. Everything else (which parts, how they are cut, the timing) is mark-up for these lines, not a second description.

- `Frame:` who or what is in the picture and where it will be seen, in one line. Decide it from the drawing; the file name is only a hint (a name may say "dancing" about a drawing that shows signals growing).
- `Motion:` the main mechanism and what answers it, as it works in real life, with an axis and a trajectory: which part turns around which point, along which path, by how much, in what beats (`opens → squeezes → hops → opens again`). Then, in the same line, how the body, head and face answer the effort, and the motion each object asks for by itself (the affordance: round things roll or spin, a wheel and a gear turn, a loader ring rotates around its center, a clock hand goes round, a pendulum and a sign swing, a door and a lid open on a hinge, a lever and a button are pressed, cloth ripples, a lamp lights up, writing is drawn on). "Swing", "sway", "wobble", "swell", "hop" without an axis and a trajectory are not accepted, and pulse or swelling never replaces the motion the object asks for. The main mechanism is decided before asking whether it is easy to cut: it is never dropped or put into `Still` because it is hard to cut. If it needs a cut, the plan says `cut` and where (B¼); an engine failure is verify's to solve, not a smaller idea.
- `Mechanism:` one phrase: how the action works in life, with the axis and the path of every part of it: what turns about what, what is cut from where (`cut`), what grows, folds or unrolls, what travels from where to where ("the wheels turn about their hubs, the foot goes round with the pedal, the leg is cut at the hip"; "the horn unrolls from the mouthpiece along its own axis"). The parts named here are decided before anyone asks how hard they are to cut and are never `— still:`.
- `Pace:` the one decision about time, made before the scene is written: `Pace: <loop>f loop, <accent>f accents, <easing>, <feel>` (`Pace: 60f loop, 12f accents, soft, playful`). Loop: the frames of the output loop (the p50 of `loopSec` of step E¼, or the style file). Accent: the frames of one push of the main motion (inside the `moveFrames` range of the style). Easing: one word for the curve most of the motion runs on: `soft` (also `inOut`, `sine`: starts from rest and settles), `out` (snap-out: fast start, soft stop; only for a thing that was already moving or a hit), `in` (a fall: a slow start, a fast arrival), `lin` (a spin, a conveyor, a drift), `spring` (a push that overshoots and settles, `back`). Feel: one of `calm` (long soft accents, no overshoot), `playful` (springs, overshoot 5–15 %), `snappy` (short accents, 8–12 frames at 30 fps), `heavy` (slow starts, 16+ frames, no bounce), `steady` (soft and linear curves, no overshoot). It does not repeat `Mechanism`: that line says what moves, this one says how it feels in time. A scene that does both (a calm sway with one snappy pop) names the feel of the main beat. The numbers go into the scene file, and step F checks the build against the line, so write what you will build, not a wish.
- `Facing:` only for a character that walks, runs or steps: `left` or `right`, the way the face and the toes look; it goes into the scene as `"facing"`, and the knee bend of the legs and the foot phase are checked against it (`common.md`, "Walk and run").
- `Why:` what the picture is about and what the motion says, one phrase ("light pleasure of a press", "the signals travel from the brain to the circuit"). Decide it yourself, don't ask the user.

Examples of the lines are in "Intent examples" at the end of this file.

The intent is the first block of scenes/<name>.story.md (step G) and is copied into `scenarios.md`. The motion plan (B¼) follows it and doesn't contradict it: the main mechanism every part named in `Mechanism` moves in it, and every object with a motion in `Motion` has a line in the plan.

**B¼. Motion plan - everything lives.** Write scenes/<name>.plan.md: one line per part - `<part> - <motion>`. Every character gets lines for torso, waist, head, each arm (bent at its joints) and the legs; every object and decor element gets its own line (trophy, road, speed lines, sparkles). The static parts are the plan's own lines, each with its reason: each part either moves with a reason or stands for a named reason (`— still: static object, a flask`, `— still: holds the sheet`, `— still: hidden behind the desk`, `— whole: fused with the phone, moves as one block with a sway and a bob`). The main beat is never alone: every part it touches or that sits next to it answers with a lag of 6–9 frames (follow-through, overlap - a tap: the device dips, its screen reacts, the neighbours settle; a pop or swing lasts 10–14 frames, so an answer 3–4 frames behind reads as the same beat, the judge fails it as one_at_a_time; 2–3 frames is only the push inside a mechanism), and a thing that hangs, floats or glows sways in one slow wave per loop. Fingers and tiny props act by the story or stand. Every moving part has `— why: <the beat of the story or what pushes it>`, an answer to the main beat is a why; a motion without a why is cut from the plan. **Floors (measured by `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/vigor.js" --check` on the build, calibrated on the approved scenes):** the biggest swing in the scene ≥ 60 px of 1200; the acting part ≥ 8° or ≥ 25 px (an arm or a leg of a figure: ≥ 14°, the same number as `angles.js`, taken from the approved scenes), what answers it ≥ 5° / ≥ 12 px / ≥ 115 %; at least 80 % of the plan lines move, and the moving parts cover at least 30 % of the drawing (lines with `— why: symbol` or `— why: background`, and `— still:`, are not counted: a like, a pin, a street line, a table stand). The drawing's integrity outranks these floors: a symbol or an icon may stand (`— why: symbol`), the ground and the support stand (`— why: background`; moving them needs a reason in the story), and a swing that opens a cut, crosses foreign lines of the drawing or pulls a fused block apart is a defect, never vigor. A plan below the floors is rewritten before the story: enlarge the swings and give the standing parts an answer or an affordance motion (the user's split test: ±3–4.5° of a hand and a few swelling dots read as "sluggish, little moves"). "Still" without a reason, a character whose only moving part is one hand, or a trembling swarm of small parts is a mistake: fix the plan before the story. Next to each moving part write how it is cut: `whole` (separate figure), `hinge` (pivot under an overlap) or `cut` - in that order of preference, `common.md` "Fused parts". The plan is checked again in verify.

**One loop length per scene.** End plan.md with `Loop: <op> frames; periods: <part>=<frames>, …` - one length for the whole scene and, for every repeating track (sway, breath, spin, drift), its period in frames; each period divides the length (op = a common multiple), so the repeat meets the seam in motion. A decor or background object (moon, cloud, balloon, bubbles, sparkles) moves only when its affordance or the story asks for it (a floating thing sways, a round loader spins, a bubble rises); an object with no reason to move stands (`— still: static object, a flask`), but "decor" alone is no reason: what hangs, floats, glows or sits next to the action gets its motion and its answer. A track that is a clean cycle (its period divides the length) costs nothing at the seam, so do not drop a motion that has a reason only to save loop work.

**The main mechanism is not given up silently.** A part named in `Mechanism` (the wheels, the foot on the pedal, the arm that presses) never goes to `— still:` because it is "hard to cut" or "not built in the first pass". If it has to be cut, the plan says so: `cut`, and where (a wheel disc cut from its outline, a leg below the knee with the seam pinned; drawn parts may be duplicated or deleted, `common.md`). If the engine cannot do it, that is a build error for verify to report, not a reason to shrink the brief to what is easy. `— still:` is for what is static, hidden or held. The plan is checked again in verify.

**B⅔. Mechanisms - the chain of causes first.** If the illustration is a mechanism (a ball dispenser, a gumball machine, a conveyor, gears, a lever with a load, a pipe with a flow), write the chain before the story: (1) what triggers (the lever); (2) what falls or moves first - the part the others lean on (the lowest ball over the throat); (3) who shifts after it and with what delay: the neighbours that lay on it move into the freed place 2–3 frames after it leaves, the next row 2–3 frames later (2–3 frames is the push inside the mechanism only; a gesture that runs along a row of parts - fingers, a wave - has 5–6 frames between neighbours), by 60–100 % of a diameter for the first row and 20–40 % for the second; (4) what stands (the flask, the frame, balls the event does not touch - no trembling); (5) what returns before the loop seam: the thing that left must come back or be replaced (a new ball falls in from the top and the settling wave runs down the pile; the shifted ones go back to their frame-0 places; the dropped one leaves the tray out of frame), so frame op equals frame 0. A thing that leaves goes behind the base by layer order or a mask, not by shrinking to zero. Write the chain as a `Chain:` paragraph at the end of scenes/<name>.plan.md, after the part lines. Only then the story (step C) and the timeline.

**B¾. Look up how Icons8 animators did it.** Search the catalog, never read a file whole (a style's file is 5–20 KB):
1. The same illustration: `grep -n -A3 "^## <name>$" stories/<style>.md` (the SVG file name without the extension). If it is there, this exact illustration was animated by hand: take its story, loop length and peak as the basis.
2. Similar scenes: 2–3 words from the file name (the object or the action: `courier`, `piggy`, `skateboard`) - `grep -n -i -A3 "<word>" stories/<style>.md`; if the style has nothing, `grep -rn -i -A3 "<word>" stories/`. Take the 2–3 closest entries. A style with no file in `stories/` (Plain, Little, Comic, Flexy, Notion Line Art have none): skip this step.
3. From the entries take: cycle or vignette, the loop length, what the peak is, which objects move and how.
The entry is a hint, not an order, and the last in the order of authority. It may need what the engine cannot do (replacement drawings, draw-on lines, a 3D turn): replace such moves as the style file says. The entries come from storyboards and can be wrong about short events (a jump, a toss, a turn): if an entry contradicts the drawing (the legs stand on the ground, the arm cannot reach), trust the drawing.

**C. Check the intent against the drawing.** Reread the intent with the picture open: the motion fits what is drawn (the legs stand on the ground, the arm can reach), the main mechanism is one action and the rest answers it, a symbol-attribute carries the meaning, the style's scheme and durations hold. Floating and swaying go only on top of the intent. A line that contradicts the drawing is rewritten now, not at the build.

**D. Cut the parts.** Group elements into meaningful parts (cursor, icon, hand, forearm, palm). Check each part: `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/preview.js" <svg> --els <indices> --out <style folder>/previews/<name>-<PART>.png` - all pieces of the part must be one object, with no foreign pieces. If different objects are fused in one element (`node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/els.js" <svg> --sub <number>` shows the outlines) - list in the part only the outlines it needs (`"17.0"`, `"17.1"`). If they are fused into one outline - a cut (`cuts`, `SCENE-FORMAT.md` "Cut and pinned seam"): the drawing with a grid `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/poses.js" <svg> --grid --out <style folder>/previews/<name>-grid.png`, check the cut with `poses.js <scene> --cuts --grid`, the cut-off part gets `pin`. Bendable parts get `bones.joints` along the joints. Attachment points go in `joints`. A volume that turns in 3D (a Techny coin) or a lid that opens is a morph to a second pose of the drawing (`SCENE-FORMAT.md` "Morph"); if the folder has no pose drawn, animate without the turn and write "needs pose: <what>" as the last line of `<name>.story.md`. If a person or an animal walks or runs, or lines are split between moving parts, go through the whole matching block of `common.md` ("Walking and overlaps": legs under a skirt, animal paws, white lines, tight line masks) before the first build and open the references named there; hips, knees, corners and line ends - from `els.js`/`centerline`, never by eye.

**E. Lay it out on the timeline.** The scenario limit is hard, 3 minutes: time is up - the scenario is fixed as is.

**F. Check the build.** Run `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/make.js" <style folder>/scenes/<name>.json --lottie-only --out <style folder>/.analyze-check`. Read `<style folder>/.analyze-check/<name>.status.json`: `done` - ready; `failed`/`error` - fix the scene by `issues`/`error` and repeat (up to 3 times). If the scene has a cut, also look at the seams in the strongest poses: `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/poses.js" <scene> --frames <peak frames> --box <seam> --cell 400 --out <style folder>/previews/<name>-seam.png`. If it doesn't work out, leave the scene and write the reason as the last line of `<name>.story.md`: "Build failed: …". If it built, check the rhythm: `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/rhythm.js" <style folder>/scenes/<name>.json --style <style>` (prints `skipped` when the style has no rhythm profile - then there is no rhythm to check); fix the hold and duration misses (`misses`) right away if that doesn't break the story. Then check the Pace line of the intent against the build: `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/pace.js" <style folder>/scenes/<name>.json --style <style>` (reads scenes/<name>.story.md; exit 1 prints `errors`). It fails when the line is missing or unreadable, the loop of the line is not the length of the scene, the accent differs from the median motion by more than 3 frames or 35 %, most of the motion runs on another curve than the one named, or the feel contradicts the numbers (`calm` with a 15 % overshoot, `snappy` with accents over 16 frames). Fix the scene when the line is the decision; fix the line when the scene has a reason to differ (a `Pace:` edit changes nothing else in the story); the style profile only warns. A story of the old form with no labelled lines is skipped.

**G. Write** scenes/<name>.story.md: the `Warning:` line if step A½ gave one, then the lines of the intent (step B), nothing else. No frames, parameters or part names.

The subagent returns only a list: name - ready / stopped: <why>, waiting for go / not animatable: <why>.

## 3. scenarios.md

1. For each illustration: `node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/preview.js" <svg> --out <style folder>/previews/<name>.png`.
2. Assemble `<style folder>/scenarios.md`. If the last line of `<name>.story.md` is "Build failed: …", it goes into `scenarios.md` in plain words ("Couldn't build: …"):

```
# Scenarios: <style>

## <name>
![](previews/<name>.png)
<the intent lines from <name>.story.md>
```

A scene with a warning has it as the first line of the section, above the intent: "Warning: stop, we won't cope - <reason>"; the summary of the run (section 4) lists these scenes with their reasons. For a skipped scene there is no `story.md`: the section is the picture and "Skipped (SVG): <what to prepare>" or "Not animatable: <reason>". When any SVG was put aside by the SVG check, `scenarios.md` ends with the section `## SVG to prepare`: one line per file - what is in it and what to do before export.

3. Delete `<style folder>/.analyze-check/` and `previews/*-labels.png`, `previews/*-<PART>.png`, `previews/*-grid.png`, `previews/*-seam.png`.

## 4. Then without stopping

The user doesn't read the scenarios - the agent decides them. The whole batch is analyzed first, every scene, warnings included.

- No scene has a warning: don't ask; run the create step on this style folder right away.
- At least one scene has a warning: do not run the create step. Show the list: scene - "stop, we won't cope - <reason>" for each (and the not animatable ones with their reason), say how many scenes are ready, and wait for the person's command. "Go" (or "do it", "let's try") - run the create step on the whole style folder as usual, the warned scenes included, and name them in the create/verify summary; if they name scenes to drop, build the rest.

Name the non-animatable and skipped scenes (with the reason) in the create/verify summary.

## Intent examples

Each with its lines: what a good intent looks like (axis and trajectory named, the answer of the body, the meaning). Take the level of detail, not the mechanics: a mechanism is chosen from the drawing in front of you.

1. A woman rides a bicycle and sings.
   - `Frame:` a woman on a bicycle, singing, seen as a loop of a ride.
   - `Motion:` both wheels turn around their hubs, her near foot goes round the pedal in a circle and the knee bends with it (the leg is cut at the hip and driven by bones); the whole picture bobs 10–14 px as on bumps, notes leave her mouth one after another and drift out, the hair flutters, the body leans forward and back with the pedalling, the arm on the handlebar follows.
   - `Mechanism:` both wheels turn about their hubs, the near foot goes round with the pedal and the knee bends with it (the leg is cut at the hip, bones drive it).
   - `Pace:` 90f loop, 14f accents, soft, steady
   - `Why:` a carefree ride with a song: everything is in motion, nothing stands.
2. A woman with dumbbells.
   - `Frame:` a woman standing with a dumbbell in each hand.
   - `Motion:` a small squat (knees and torso down 20–30 px), then both arms rise and straighten at the elbow (the arms are cut at the armpits, the elbow is a joint); the eyes squint and the mouth tightens in the squat and relax at the top, the head and torso follow the effort; the motion lines by the dumbbells are not in frame 0 and appear after the arms straighten.
   - `Mechanism:` the arms are cut at the armpits and straighten at the elbow after a small squat.
   - `Pace:` 72f loop, 20f accents, soft, heavy
   - `Why:` the effort of a lift and its release: the face and the body tell it, not only the arms.
3. A man with a party horn.
   - `Frame:` a man in a shirt holding a party horn, which starts rolled up and small.
   - `Motion:` he throws his head back and then swings it forward around the neck; during the swing the blue part of the horn unrolls along its own axis (its length grows from the mouthpiece), then rolls back before the seam; the shirt collar shifts a few pixels with the swing.
   - `Mechanism:` the head swings about the neck; the blue part of the horn unrolls from the mouthpiece along its own axis and rolls back before the seam.
   - `Pace:` 60f loop, 12f accents, out, snappy
   - `Why:` the blow of a party horn: the horn unrolls at the moment of the push.
4. A piggy bank with a coin.
