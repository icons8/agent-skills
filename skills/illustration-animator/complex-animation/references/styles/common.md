# Common animation rules

`${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}` is the folder that holds this SKILL.md and `references/` (the engine is in `../engine`); engine commands, document paths and clients that leave the variable as is: "Paths" in `SKILL.md`.

The source is Icons8 animations; this is a distillation of them. It takes priority over any external principles. A style file (styles/<style>.md) and its `*.lessons.md` refine these rules: their "User bans" and "Lessons" outrank this file (the user's ban on a style is stronger than any number from a measured profile, see "Style profiles" in the analyze step).

## Hard rules

- Every animation has a plot: a short story with cause and effect. At least one action (`action`) and a reaction to it (`reaction`).
- One action at a time: one click - one reaction. Two reactions of different parts do not start closer than 3 frames to each other (the script floor); pops and swings that last 10–14 frames - 6 frames or more, otherwise the judge reads one beat.
- Floating and swaying (`ambient`) - only as part of the plot, on top of an action. An animation made of floating alone is forbidden. Exception: what flies by nature (balloons and what they carry, a ghost) - its plot motion is the float itself: smooth and slow on sine, no jerk, spring or squash; the reactions are a pendulum sway of the load and the balloons in turn.
- On holds the character is still, unless the style rules allow otherwise (Plain - body sway as part of the plot, Comic - drift of ~5 frames after a pose). Symbols, interface and decor live on background motion.
- The illustration (SVG) is a key pose somewhere in the loop. The loop can start at any phase (`start` in the scene). Symbols outside their moment are hidden.
- The loop is seamless. Geometry from the SVG is carried over one to one: we move, rotate, scale, bend on bones.
- Motion templates are a starting point. Parameters are tuned for each scene.
- We do not do line boil.

## Affordance - the motion an object asks for

Before the plot, decide for each object by its shape and role how it moves on its own: a round thing rolls or spins, a wheel, gear, loader ring and spinner rotate around the center (`spin`), a clock hand goes around in a circle, a pendulum and a sign swing (`swing`), a door and a lid open on a hinge (pivot on the hinge), a lever and a button are pressed, a spring compresses, cloth ripples. The plot is built from these motions. Pulse and swelling - only for what itself "breathes" (a heart, a lamp, a notification); they do not replace rotation or travel: a round loader is a rotation of the whole ring, not a wave of swellings across the segments.

## Review notes: 2D test: Little, Neat, Comic, Plain - takes priority over the rest

The user rejected all 20 scenes of the test ("overall it is all bad"); the judge had passed 13 of them. Most points below were already rules and were skipped - they are mandatory, and the judge fails a scene that breaks any of them.

- **The whole character lives, not only the acting hand.** Torso, waist, head, both arms and the legs each get a motion: the acting arm swings at shoulder, elbow and wrist with chain lag (the big gesture is a rotation of the whole cut-off limb or its segment about the joint; a knee or an elbow that has to bend visibly is bent on bones, "Walk and run - the knee bends"); the other arm answers (a swing, a counter-move); the torso and waist lean and bounce with the gesture; the legs bend at the knees or shift weight; the head follows with lag. A part stays still only if it's hidden or can't be moved without drawing in what the picture doesn't have - write that down in the motion plan; "hard to cut" is not a reason, and a part of the main mechanism never stays still. "No laziness": a girl with a flag whose second arm, torso and legs stand still is a fail. Cut a limb off with a mask to move it (a raised arm with a controller: cut it, bend it at the elbow, move the head more).
- **A continuous gesture is one continuous cycle.** Waving a flag, a scooter ride, walking - a steady loop through the whole animation, not bursts of "wave-like twitching" with stops.
- **Rubbery, not shaky.** One large soft swing with squash on contact and a cartoon bounce after it (a high-five: the slap squashes both hands, then they spring apart), never several small fast jerks. A vehicle rocks smoothly up and down, it doesn't shake "as on potholes". `make.js` warns `jitter` (in `warnings`) for a part that trembles - irregular small swings several times a second; it misses shake made of several parts reacting at once, so watch the sheet too.
- **Every motion has a reason; an answer to the main beat is a reason.** A soft motion with no event behind it (breathing, a hanging sway, lag behind what drives it) belongs to everything that hangs, floats or sits next to the action - a torso, a prop, a screen, a pin, a pennant - one slow wave per loop, never a tremble. Fingers and tiny props either act by the story or stand: write `— still: <why>` in the plan (a static object: a flask of balls, a table, a wall, a machine body; a hand that holds the sheet). Every motion in the plan carries a `— why:` (the beat of the story or what pushes it); a motion without a why is cut. "Moves" is not "shakes": a small motion never trembles, and fingers that stir after the tap read as typing. What the event touches answers it, as a causal chain: the thing hit moves first, what leans on it follows 2–3 frames later, the next row later still (the 2–3 frame delay is only for the push inside a mechanism: balls, gears, a lever; a gesture that runs along a row - fingers, a wave - keeps 5–6 frames between neighbours, 2–3 frames at 30 fps is 10 beats a second, the rhythm of a keyboard); what the event does not touch may stand. Trembling or shaking with no cause (a swarm of balls jittering, props twitching) is a defect. Motion strokes and speed lines appear and disappear in the loop along the action (a runner's lines come out behind him as he runs).
- **Affordance - how the thing works in life.** Clock hands are separate parts that move independently; a man lying on a hand pushes it, straining. An umbrella opens, then the rain starts, then it folds. Writing and drawing on paper is drawn on (strokes appear one after another), never scaled; paper and what's printed on it don't jiggle - it's not an interface. A team's hands come together and stack one on top of another. A thrown plane flies out along its path and stays whole, leaving past the frame edge - it never vanishes cut off or shrinks to zero in mid-air. Small details (a binocular focus knob) don't spin for no reason; don't swell random parts. An object that can't really turn in 2D (a disco ball's facets) isn't faked by sliding its texture - it squashes and wobbles by scale, the sparkles pop.
- **No tears at any frame.** A part scaled on its own comes away from what it's attached to (a hand from the arm): scale the whole limb, or put the pivot at the junction. Cut fingers bend on bones or stay with the hand - no crease at the cut. The neck junction, the trousers at the waistband, the arm at the shoulder stay closed in the strongest poses - check them with `poses.js --frames … --box …` before the build is delivered.
- **What pops out of a container isn't chopped by its mask.** Groceries jumping out of a cart: the cart's mask covers only below the rim; above the rim the object is whole.
- **Inside the canvas.** The moving object stays fully inside the frame at its extreme pose (a plane's tip at the surge peak) - reduce the travel rather than clip.
- **Perspective by scale.** An object coming toward the viewer: its near part grows more than the far part (binocular lenses faster than the body), with a small shift; there is no 3D camera.

## Review notes: brief test: the user's brief against the analyzer's

- **The main mechanism outranks neatness.** A scene whose wheels spin and whose foot goes round with the pedal beats a clean one in which the bicycle only rocks as one piece. The part that makes the action work is built first, the rest around it.
- **A secondary motion doesn't argue with the main one.** An ear, a collar, a tuft of hair: a small amplitude (a few degrees), in time with the main motion and after it; the big ear swing of a piggy bank competes with the coin and the hop.

## Loop - one mini-story

- Vignette: 3–10 beats, holds between them, a return stroke; palindrome or A–A–B.
- Cycle: walking, running, swinging, flight, riding, pulsing in a circle; no holds, the event on top of the cycle. If the illustration allows a continuous looped story (a rocket flies, a car drives, a dashed line runs in a circle and the figures move in time with it) - make it: that is a plot too.
- A simple illustration (one symbol without a character: a lamp, an icon in a bubble) - a simple loop without a plot is acceptable: the main symbol "breathes" (smoothly swells and subsides), small elements around it scale one after another in a circle. Parts do not move in time with each other: each has its own period (a divisor of the loop, so the seam is seamless), peaks are spread apart in phase; nested parts do not inherit the parent's scale.
- Almost always there is an animated attribute symbol: a star, a battery, Zzz, a heart, "?", a cloud, a paper plane, a note, a checkmark, a chart. The symbol is the main carrier of meaning.
- The ending returns to the pose at the start of the loop, often through a spring.
- Symbols around the main object: the first frame of the output loop is only the main object (a coupon, a card, a closed door), symbols appear one at a time toward the middle, hold, and toward the end leave one at a time, the last one before the seam. Key pose = SVG, the needed loop start is set through `start`.
- **The first frame of the loop does not have to match the SVG.** If in the plot the illustration is the peak of an action (circles from a touch, a heart, checkmarks, confetti, a raised hand), the loop starts from the state before it: the circles are not there yet and the finger is raised, there is no heart, the checkboxes are empty and the pencil lies down, one cone is visible. The peak (the SVG pose) is in the middle of the loop, then a return to the initial state. Set through `start` and hiding symbols; key pose = SVG remains the author's frame 0, and the `frame0` check is run against it.
- A gesture-state (waves, breathes, sways) goes as a continuous cycle through the whole loop, and events (symbols appearing) go on top of it; a single gesture with a return reads as a reset.
- A figure whose legs are merged with the body into one outline steps or runs only if the legs can be cut off whole without showing what the drawing does not have; the cut edges follow our edge rules (butt joint at the junction, overlap under the neighbor, tight line masks - "Walking and overlaps"). If they can't be cut off whole, no step: peek out and hide, hop, tilt of the torso (the torso bends, the feet are pinned to the floor - "Fused parts").

## Smoothness

The smoothness reference for all styles without their own profile is Plain (rules in `plain.md`).
- Large parts (character, head, shield, car) do not move in jerks: head turn 8–12 frames, tilt and gesture 10–20, travel 15–34; deceleration longer than acceleration (`[0.3, 0, 0.15, 1]`), spring 13–38 frames.
- Fast (2–6 frames) - only pop-ups and clicks of small symbols.
- Almost all the time something is moving, but softly: a long smooth motion is better than a short sharp one.
- Smooth does not mean slow: the tempo is like Plain (gesture 10–20 frames; the loop length is below), extra holds and stretched motions are not needed. Take the short end of the motion ranges for one-off motions (holds - per the style table, do not cut them; travel of a large part - not shorter than the distance dictates, see "Duration grows slower than distance"): in review the user asked again and again to speed up (shield, collaboration ×2, premium, door ×2), never to slow down. **A repeating gesture (a wave, breathing, a sway) takes the long end: a half-swing of a large part is 18–24 frames, an in-out breath 3–4 s (90–120 frames); a hand that waves faster than ~1.5 swings a second reads as a flutter.** Loop length: a one-off scene (one character with one action, one symbol, one object) is 2 s (60 frames at 30 fps), 6–12 frames between keys; a scene whose main motion repeats is as long as its repeating gesture needs (2 swings of 18–24 frames each is 80–96 frames, a breath 3–4 s). A scene with two story beats - up to 3 s; 4 s and more only for a cycle with an event on top. The style file may set its own length where it comes from the style's own references (Plain, Comic, Flexy, Notion Line Art).
- Smooth does not mean small (a scene whose biggest swing is under 60 px of 1200 reads as sluggish - `vigor.js --check` flags it): amplitudes stay large (hop 20–60 px, tilt 5–15°, swelling 115–130 %), time is stretched, the range is not reduced. **Shortening the loop never shrinks the swing:** if the loop got shorter, the amplitude, the cuts and the chain lag stay as they were; an already approved version of the scene has its amplitudes and cuts as a lower bound. A body that moved 50 px in a 3.2 s loop does not move 12 px in 2 s.
- **The loop is continuous.** Background and cyclic tracks cross the seam in motion: a sine with a phase, or keys with the `through` curve - not an ease to a stop at the end of the loop and a start from rest at the beginning. The whole frame never rests at the seam (the check `loop` is an error when a long rest ends in a burst at the seam, and a warning for a long held frame, an energy jump across the seam and a period that does not divide the loop). The loop length is a common multiple of the periods of its tracks. "Rest on frame 0 and on `op`" means the shape is the drawing as in the SVG there, not that the whole frame has zero speed: a slow idle (a breath, a sway) keeps running through the seam.
- **There is no frame where everything stands at once**, except a pause planned in the story. A part that crosses its rest pose in the middle of a swing crosses it at speed: the engine does it by default for `soft`/`inOut` keys (the tangent at a key comes from its neighbours, zero speed only at the edges of the swing, also across the loop seam); `sine`, `lin` and custom curves opt out. The edges of neighbouring parts' swings are apart by at least a quarter of the period, or the parts have different periods (balloons 20, 30 and 60 frames, signs alternating) - not all stopping together at the seam.

## Fused parts

When a character is fused into one outline with an object or with itself (a bartender with the counter, an arm with the torso, a horse's leg with the body, as in Journal), it is cut and bent (`cuts` and `pin` in `SCENE-FORMAT.md`). Don't filter such illustrations out. The exception is people while the skill skips them (see the people gate of the analyze step): a person fused with the torso or the floor is skipped there.

- **Where to cut - in this order.** (1) The part is already a separate closed figure in the SVG (a raised forearm with its hand, a lever, a lid): do not cut, move it whole. (2) A hinge under an overlap: the base of the part goes under another figure (a forearm under the arm lying on the table) - put the pivot there, the overlapping figure hides the joint, no cut and no seam. (3) Only then a cut with a pinned seam (below). Write the case (1, 2 or 3) for every moving part in the plan; a cut where (1) or (2) was possible is a mistake: it caps the angle at 4–10° and tears.
- **Cut only what moves.** An arm that reaches - cut at the shoulder; a character that sways - cut from the counter or the floor. Leave the rest alone.
- **Big gesture - rotate the cut-off limb; bones bend the joint.** The large swing of a cut-off arm, leg or torso is a rotation of the whole limb (or its segment) about the joint, with chain lag. Bones (`bones`, `bend`) deform the part: a rubbery bend of 40–70° at a knee or an elbow is allowed (the inside of the bend squeezes by up to 35% of the area); `lint` (`deform`) rejects only a path that folds over itself - then the bend is too strong or the joints are in the wrong place. A chain of rigid pieces with round joints gives steps at bends over 10°, and in an outline style the line breaks already at 8°. A part with `pin` loses about 10% of its area at a rotation of 25° against the pin - that is normal.
- **The seam is pinned.** Every cut-off part has `pin`: points on the cut line (`line`) stand still, the motion grows over 100–250 px (`feather`: a short part - 100–120, a torso - 200–320). Support points are pinned too: feet on the floor, a palm on the counter (`zone` or the same `line`).
- **No drawing in of what is hidden.** Up to ~15–20° at a joint, a torso tilt of 3–6° (that is for a torso cut from a fused outline with a pinned seam; a torso that is a separate part follows the amplitudes of the style). What is hidden isn't in the drawing and can't be drawn in: don't take stories where a part travels far and reveals the hidden (the bartender steps out from behind the counter, an arm comes out from behind the back). A leg that is whole in the drawing (hip to boot) walks: see "Walk and run - the knee bends". Replace such a story with one that rests on a bend and a tilt, and make a symbol the main event. The ban is on showing what the picture does not have. Parts that are already drawn may be duplicated, moved and deleted for the motion: the user cuts a leg at the front, deletes the rear piece and duplicates the front piece backward to make a pedalling pair; a wheel disc is cut out of its outline to spin. Allowed: extending an existing line or limb in a redrawn copy (`svg-extra`) where the extension continues the drawing or stays under other parts (the witch's legs, the astronaut's line; see "Matching the SVG" and the lines about `svg-extra` below).
- **Never bend a whole fused figure with bones without a cut:** bone weights leak into the neighbouring parts (the counter, the other arm), the motion comes out robotic.
- **Check the seams by eye.** `poses.js --cuts` - what moved where; `poses.js --frames … --box …` - the seam in the strongest poses. `make.js` catches a seam visible at rest (`cut`) and a tear in motion (`tear`).

Moving a character as a rigid whole silhouette (tilt, hop, sway) when the plot rests on a body pose (dance, squat, run) looks robotic - move its parts at the joints (arms, legs, torso): rotate the cut-off limbs about the joint, bend knees and elbows on bones, cutting where needed.

## Matching the SVG

After every build - in analyze before animation and in verify after every fix - the key pose frame is matched against the SVG: all elements in place, the layer order is the same, nothing is covered by the wrong thing. `make.js` checks this itself (`structure`, `frame0`); a missing or covered element is a scene error, not a style feature.

## Motion

- **Appearance** - scale 0 → 100 % over 2–6 frames with an overshoot of +3…+7 % (small ones up to +18 %), settle 2–5 frames. We almost never use opacity.
- **Disappearance** is faster than appearance: 4–6 frames, a swell up to +24 % before collapsing is allowed.
- **Overshoot and settle.** A large motion ends with an overshoot of 15–30 % of the amplitude and a settle over 2–5 frames. Spring: each swing is 0.3–0.4 times smaller than the previous one, half-period 4–8 frames.
- **Anticipation.** Before a large motion - a wind-up in the opposite direction.
- **Hierarchy lag.** `bend` with 2+ bones has `lag` 1 by default. 1–2 frames per level (shoulder → forearm → hand → fingers; torso → neck → head). Siblings in a staircase +1. Chain ends (hair, tails, earrings) lag by 3–12 frames and swing 1.5–2 times more than the parent.
- **Linked event** (a symbol, a reaction of the surroundings) starts together with the gesture or 1–3 frames later. Successive departures of elements - about 9 frames apart.
- **Easing.** Linear - only for uniform motion (passing through the frame, a conveyor, constant rotation: `constant: true`). Everything else - with zero velocity at the extremes, deceleration usually longer than acceleration. A fall is pure acceleration; surfacing and landing - deceleration plus a spring.
- **Start from rest - only with a flat start.** From rest (after a hold, from the loop start) - `soft` [0.3,0,0.15,1] (as in Plain), `inOut` or `sine`. `out` and `back` start 4.5 times faster than the average speed - that is a jerk; they suit only the continuation of a motion already underway (speed before the key ≈ speed after). The exception is a snap of up to 8 frames (a pop, a click, a rebound after an impact, a squash released): it may start with `out` - the user approved such accents. Speeds on both sides of a key converge: a kink of more than 2 times and arriving at a key at full speed (`hit at the key`) are rejected by `make.js` (`speed`).
- **Overshoot and settle - by spring.** Not by hand keys "overshoot → undershoot → rest", but by a damped spring: the `pop`, `slide`, `bounce` templates are already like that; after your own motion - the `spring` template (`zeta` 0.28–0.36, half-period `half` 4–8 frames, `v0` - the speed of the incoming motion).
- **Arcs.** Living travel goes along an arc, not along a ruler: `arc` 0.1–0.2 for `move` and `slide` (a hand, a jump, a symbol's flight). Straight - only UI (a toggle, a slider, a cursor along a row).
- **Duration grows slower than distance.** 100 px - ×1, 200 px - ×1.3, 400 px - ×1.6 (Plain: 230 → 460 px in 27 → 34 frames; at 100 px ~20 frames). Rhythm checks this (`move duration to distance`).
- **Parts do not run into each other.** Parts that are apart in the key pose must not overlap each other in motion by more than 15 % (`overlap`). An intended touch (a cursor on a button, palm on palm) - `touch` on the part or an action → reaction pair.

## Durations (frames at 30 fps; at 24 fps multiply by 0.8)

| What | Frames |
|---|---|
| Micro-motion, jerk, click | 2–9 |
| Symbol appearance | 2–6 |
| Blink | 4–7 (closing shorter than opening) |
| Gesture, head turn | 8–20 |
| Slow tilt, "tired" motion | 20–30 |
| Hold in a vignette | 4–20 |
| Step | 12–20 (tired - 32) |

## What the engine cannot reproduce and what to replace it with

- Facial expression, mouth, hand gesture change, three-quarter head turns - we do not depict them unless they are in the SVG.
- Blink - scaling the eye vertically.
- A stroke being drawn - strokes appear one at a time.
- Going under water - squeezing to the waterline or disappearing.
- 3D turn - horizontal flip 100 → 0 → −100 %.
- Hair physics - keys with a lag of 1–2 frames.
- There are no cameras, blurs or glows anywhere.

## Easing in a scene

Engine names: `soft` [0.3,0,0.15,1] (default for `move`, `turn`, `bend`), `inOut` [0.65,0,0.35,1], `out` [0.22,1,0.36,1], `in` [0.5,0,0.75,0.5], `back` [0.34,1.5,0.64,1], `sine` [0.37,0,0.63,1], `lin`. From rest - only `soft`, `inOut`, `sine` (`out`/`back` jerk at the start). Any key also accepts its own curve `[x1,y1,x2,y2]` (x1, x2 from 0 to 1). Style curves are in the style files.

## Review notes: Little, Rough Sketch - takes priority over the rest

- "Speed up" = cut the stretches where almost nothing happens (holds, empty pauses, stretched wind-ups), not drive the motions faster. A simple vignette with a simple plot - a 2 s loop (see "Smoothness"), no dead spots.
- The animation is "rubbery" and smooth: overshoot and spring after every hit, touch and click; squash & stretch by scale on whoever got hit or took the hit (a magnet on touching money, a knot on a click, a hand on a clap). Clumsy = a rigid turn of a piece without spring and lag.
- The character must look alive: torso, head, shoulders and arms move together with the gesture (chain lag, breathing, tilt), not only the hand or the symbol balls.
- Affordance is strict: a car drives (everything sways up and down together), a pencil puts checkmarks, a ghost flies (levitation up and down), electrons fly along their orbits, gears all rotate (as a pair, in opposite directions), a tape measure extends. A character standing on the ground does not levitate.
- A center that by meaning stands still (an atom's nucleus, a block) does not rotate.
- Lines that cross during motion (legs in walking, bends) are hidden with dynamic masks (`maskBy`), not left sticking out (see "Walking and overlaps").
- Bend arms and legs at the joints rubbery, with chain lag; running and walking - not a rigid pendulum.

## Review notes

- Deadlines are hard: script per illustration - 3 minutes, animation - 7 minutes, judge - 3 minutes per scene (one round, advice only), fixes of script findings - 4 minutes. At the start of a stage take the time (`node -e "console.log(Date.now())"`, milliseconds: works in every shell, PowerShell included), check it before each new round of fixes. Time is up - stop and deliver the best assembled version; there are no extensions and no "one more attempt". Write its deadline into the prompt of every subagent (animator, fixer, judge). Do not compare frame by frame. The judge gives advice in one round and nothing is rebuilt on its remarks; then deliver.
- What is not in the SVG is not drawn with a white backing and not "redrawn" anew: cut the missing part out of existing geometry (tape measure - cut a piece off the existing tape and extend it, do not insert a foreign strip). Continuing an existing line or limb in `svg-extra` is not "redrawing": it is allowed where the continuation stays under other parts or continues the drawing in a straight line (see "No drawing in").
- If the needed pose of an object is not drawn in the SVG, the object is rotated or extended: paper money turns flat toward the magnet, the tape pulls out of the housing.
- Something rose and the needed line is missing in the SVG (the arm is raised, the torso is left without an outline) - draw the line in (a copy of the SVG with an additional stroke, hidden in the SVG pose).
- Two parts moving along their own orbits must not stick together: different electrons have different phases and periods, in one frame their centers are ≥ a diameter apart.
- A character peeking out, exiting and appearing - smooth, with a wind-up and a spring, not an abrupt poking out (organic door).
- Do not overcomplicate: if the plot is levitation, blinking and light stirring of the arms (a ghost), that is all, without a scatter of events.
- The best of today's scenes is the skeleton runner (rough-sketch-spooky-skeleton…): running with a bouncing pelvis, bends with lag, rubbery arms. The reference of rubberiness for characters.

## Review notes: body and leg outlines

- A raised arm does not "hang" separately: the line of the torso's side from the armpit down stays in place, and the underside of the raised arm meets it at the armpit. The correct frame: the arm is raised, the torso's side is intact, the lower line of the arm runs into the side. The wrong one: the torso's side and the arm have drifted apart, the lower stroke of the arm ends in the air.
- Two legs in walking always read as two legs. In the passing pose they do not merge into one pillar with doubled and broken trouser lines. The key step pose - legs wide apart (the correct frame), the intermediate ones - a smooth transition between the wide step and the passing pose, where the legs are slightly offset from each other, not coinciding.
- The trouser line of each leg is whole from hip to shoe in every frame. Intersections are not cured by losing pieces of line (scraps, shortened strokes): spread the legs apart by phase, change the layer order.

## Review notes: lines at a bend

- If something bends and a line pulls away from another, we animate that line: it lengthens or shortens and is always brought to the place where it was joined (armpit, crotch, knee). The body and outline are not deformed: lines move and stretch, not the shape.
- Astronaut: the torso's side stays vertical in place, as in the SVG, the lower line of the raised arm stretches to it. The torso does not turn into a triangle.

## Walking and overlaps (reference - the hiker) - takes priority over the old items

Walking references - the hiker and the skeleton: `examples/rough-sketch/scenes/rough-sketch-hiker-walking-with-backpack-and-trekking-poles-among-pine-trees.json` and `…/rough-sketch-spooky-skeleton-figure-running-forward-with-outstretched-arm-gesture.json`. Before your own walk, open their scenes.

- Moving limbs are cut off from the body as separate parts (the whole leg with the shoe, its own pivot at the hip - at the outer upper corner of the trouser leg, so the top does not come away from the waistband).
- What goes behind one another hides under the dynamic mask of the near part (`maskBy`, `out: true`). The near one is the one whose line runs straight through at the junction, while the other's line butts into it (T-junction). The far leg's shoe gets its own mask by the traced outline of the near shoe (`poly`), not by the convex hull.
- A line that sticks out past the waistband on rotation is cut off by a mask at the waistband (`maskBy` without `out` on the torso).
- Line breaks are drawn in with a dynamic line (a copy of the SVG with an additional stroke in `svg-extra`, as with the astronaut); an object butting into a limb (a pole) is attached to it at the point of contact (`parent`, pivot at the point) and subtracts its rotation.
- The ends of lines cut by a mask are round, like a pen's (the engine puts them itself on a part sawn off by a mask on itself); the cut goes across the lines, never along (along - splinters and stubs).
- A short stub seam at the junction of sawn-off parts (the crotch) is not given to either part; a hole in the fill is closed with the figure's own fill at the very bottom.
- The near leg of a runner in three-quarter view - by the side rule below (faces right - near is left); when in doubt - whose line runs straight through at the junction.
- Running is not "scissors" (both legs together converge and diverge, reads as a side-step): the legs swap places, the leading leg in the swing rises (the top goes under the torso), the support leg stands on the ground; the torso is lowest on the support, higher in flight.
- Layer order of limbs on a person in profile: faces right - the limbs that are to the left of the body in the picture are ABOVE the body, those to the right - UNDER the body; faces left - the opposite. This applies to arms, legs and details on them.
- A T-junction is a check of the same thing: the part whose line butts into the other at the junction is the one underneath (the lower line of the arm butts into the shirt's side - the arm is under the torso). Lead the cut right up to the line that is butted into: the whole line of the limb leaves with it, and that line stays whole (otherwise a stub with a tip hangs on the body). Limb details (sleeve stripes, a cuff) go under the body together with it - as a copy in `svg-extra`, otherwise they are drawn on top.
- Tight line mask (`maskBy.lines`): the mask hugs the stroke itself tightly, by its thickness, with round ends where the line is cut off; no convex hull and no outline traced with a margin. Variant A (`fill: true`) - the strokes plus the interior of the limb, the open side closed by a straight chord between the ends: the limb covers what is behind it. Variant B (without `fill`) - only the lines: cuts them off with round ends, covers nothing. A limb drawn as one outline with the body (a hanging arm in the witch's dress) is sawn off like this: the body - `out: true` by the arm's lines, variant B (only its lines leave the body, the fill stays), a copy of the body in `svg-extra` - variant A by the same lines. The ends are led under the neighboring detail (a cape, a hem), the chord also under it. The ends can be moved along the line (`trim`) - the line lengthens and shortens on the step. Leg fills (chords and `add`) must meet the torso cut and each other without gaps, otherwise a transparent triangle shows under the hem; the gap at the crotch is closed by the figure's own fill without lines, and the crotch stub stays in no part - the near leg's line ends at the T-junction (loader runner). References for line masks - the witch with the cat and the loader runner: `examples/ballpoint-pen/scenes/` (their redrawn copies in `svg-extra/`).
- Torso on tight line masks too (loader runner): every cut line ends round and lines meet at junctions without gaps. Torso, limbs and the line between them end at the same junction point (round ends overlap). A short side line that runs from the waist to under a raised arm belongs to the arm, and the arm turns about the middle of that line: its bottom then slides along the waistline and the shoulder line slides along itself, so neither junction opens; start the arm's shoulder line 30–40 px into the torso's line. A line that lies on another contour of the compound path (the waistline) is found only from points on that line itself, away from junctions; check the found width (a thin waistline vs the thick outline). When a line moves from one part to another, move the white fills too: the upper part's fill (chord and `add`) stops 6–8 px short of the lines that now belong to the part below it, otherwise its straight edge shows over that part (a hard edge across the sleeve). Where two white fills meet, the lower part's `add` reaches 10–20 px under the upper one: two coincident anti-aliased edges leave a hairline seam. Check fills over every frame of the loop, not only the key pose. A white line split at its corner between body and leg (cat): if the leg also shifts (lift on the swing), the body piece gets the same shift, otherwise the line tears at the corner. Loop seam check: frame op equals frame 0 by keys (lint `seam`), and the pixel change across 63→0 must equal the change at other step keys (31→32); frame op itself is outside the loop and must not be rendered. Do not split a white line with a straight-edged mask: at any bend the cut corners stick out past the round dot (a kink). Redraw each piece in `svg-extra` as a round-capped stroke along the line's center (both pieces end at the exact corner center found with `centerline`), so the round ends coincide at every angle.
- Animals (the witch's cat): walk with a diagonal gait - near hind with far front, then far hind with near front; the paw in the swing rises; swing about ±16°. The paws of a filled silhouette are cut off as separate vectors (a limb is a separate vector, as an artist rigs it), not by masking copies of the body: the body without them, each of the four paws moves on its own; the far paws are under the body, rotating at the top. A hind leg bends at the knee: the thigh stays, the shin turns. A white dividing line is cut at its corner into two pieces - the one along the body stays, the one along the paw moves with it - and the paw's axis goes into that corner: the line bends instead of tearing or sticking out (a white dot on the bend).
- No white backing (`backfill`) and no trimming lines by scaling to zero.
- Step: the legs converge to parallel and diverge to the SVG pose (rigid legs - about ±14°; a straight leg may stay rigid unless the style asks for a bend at the knee - Little does), do not go deep behind one another; the torso bounces on the pass.
- Short legs under a skirt or dress (the witch): "the knees need to be extended" - draw the legs longer upward under the dress (`svg-extra`: a fill and two capsule lines continuing the sides of the leg), the axis is in the new hip, then the hips are close together and a straight leg walks by a rotation of ±40° with a shift of ±60, as in the loader: the legs swap places, the leading one in the swing rises. Otherwise the top of the leg sticks out from under the hem as a "box", and the legs break.
- White dividing lines on a filled silhouette (the cat's paws) are extended to the silhouette's edge - with drawn-in capsule ends, otherwise on the paw's rotation the line breaks off inside.
- A leg extension continues the leg's sides strictly in a straight line: take the direction from the last 15–20 centerline points at the end of each stroke, same width, not by eye - otherwise a "knee" appears at the seam (user: "broke the legs at the knees again").
- A straight leg turns as one rigid piece with its boot: never counter-rotate the boot - any opposite turn breaks the shin at the bootleg edge. On the pass the whole leg lifts along an arc.
- `bounds` in lint checks a part's rotated bounding box, not its drawing: a rotating boot can report a false exit off the canvas - confirm with the pixels at the frame edge.
- Where the body line was cut out from under a sawn-off arm, the remaining line (the dress corner under the fingers) gets its own round end: a circle the thickness of the line in `svg-extra` under the arm, at the edge of the cutout.

## Vigor (split test, Black Chalk)

- A fused hand-and-object outline moves as one block (`whole`: sway ≥ 3°, hop ≥ 10 px); the old "still: fused" turned a hand, a phone and a pin into a standing picture with ±3° - read as "sluggish". `vigor.js --check` floors: biggest swing ≥ 60 px, moving parts ≥ 30 % of the drawing, ≥ 80 % of plan lines move.
- An old fused outline that tears under a cut is moved whole, with the small detail (a ring, a dot) separately; do not stop it.
- A plan line with a `— why:` of its own is the only licence to stand; decor beside the action answers it with a slow wave.
- The loop seam allows a slow bob on one property and a reaction on another (`p` vs `r`) on the same part; a long rest after the burst is the loop warning, the judge fails `loop_reads` on it.
- The frame-0 compare never stretches the render to the SVG size (a non-square SVG gave a false 18 %).
- **The drawing's integrity outranks vigor** (user: a like "too active, falls apart"; a hand "torn lines, crosses the trackpad line"; a street line "wobbles for no reason"). A symbol, icon or UI mark (like, pin, badge) may stand - `— why: symbol`; the ground and the support (a street, a map, the earth, a table, a wall) stand - `— why: background`; `vigor.js --check` does not ask them to move and does not count them in the share of moving parts. Moving the background needs a reason in the story (wind, the camera, a conveyor) - otherwise a wobbling street line is a defect.
- The swing of a fused block is capped from above: the cut stays closed (no torn line ends visible at the seam), the part does not cross other lines of the drawing unless the story puts it above them (a hand over a trackpad line), and the block does not fall apart (the ceiling is measured in `vigor.js --check` per block, from the approved scenes). Motion that pulls fused parts apart or breaks the silhouette is a defect, however lively it looks: cut the swing down before the next build.

## Review notes: no-judge test: lines of the drawing

- **A moving part does not step onto the foreign lines of the drawing** (user on a thumb over a frame: "the finger crosses the frame lines, bad"; on a hand over a trackpad). A hand, a finger or a prop passes in front of a line only when the story puts it above that line and the line is hidden under the part's fill; otherwise shorten the swing or move the pivot until the part stays on its own side. A drawing drawn above the line in the SVG order is not a licence.
- **An outline cut off for motion is extended at the break, frame by frame** (user: "if we cut something off, extend the outlines at the break frame by frame"; "line breaks are not ok"). The ends of the cut lines stay closed and never hang free at any frame: bend the cut on bones so the outline stays one line, or hide the seam under a dense line-mask (`maskBy.lines`) or a redrawn copy (`svg-extra`) that continues the line past the break; if neither holds at the swing you need, reduce the swing, and do not ship a visible gap.
- **Where the machines look.** `make.js` stops a cut whose line shows in motion as a thin dark line the drawing does not have at rest (`hair`), and the status lists as `lineend` / `lineover` warnings the places where the end of a line came off the lines it stopped at or the lines of two parts meet anew; `sheet.js` cuts those places out as `places.png` (the rest frame beside the frame of the place) with `places.md`. Open `places.png` before you hand a scene over: a line that ends in the air where it ran into another one at rest, a gap in an outline, a part lying on foreign lines are the user's rejects whatever the angle floors say - the lines of the drawing outrank the swing floor of `angles.js` and the plan's amplitudes; cut the swing, not the line.
- **A cut line does not run along a stroke.** A cut whose line goes along a stroke at a sharp angle leaves the lower piece, which tucks 3 px under the upper one, peeking out along the seam as a thin dark sliver once the pieces part (a globe held in a hand: the pieces of the hand "fell off"); the tuck grows by itself where the seam opens (`ov`, up to 12 px) but cannot hide it. Put the cut line across the strokes (square to them where it crosses the drawing), or at a place where the line is hidden under a fill, or do not cut.
## Walk and run - the knee bends

A stiff leg that swings like a pendulum is not a walk or a run; where the knee is visible it bends, in both directions. This section is mandatory for every character that walks, runs, steps or pedals (a squat, a bob or a hop on planted feet is judged by its own story, not by this section: the knee needs only to give with the body, and the depth of a squat is never raised to satisfy a number), and the judge fails a cycle whose knee does not visibly flex (`node "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engine/bin/angles.js"` shows the largest bend: under ~35° for a walk or ~60° for a run is "low").

- **Cut the leg at the hip, bend it at the knee.** Whole leg in the drawing (hip to boot in one outline): one part with `bones.joints` hip → knee → ankle and a `bend` track (a rubbery bend of 40–70° is within the `deform` limit), the pivot at the hip, the boot rides at the end of the chain. Thigh and shin drawn as separate figures: two parts, the shin is a child of the thigh with the pivot at the knee, the pieces overlap 20–50 px under a round cap, the thigh's lower end hides the joint. Take the hip, knee and ankle from the geometry of the SVG (the outer top corner of the trouser leg, the middle of the leg's visible length or the knee shape, the top of the boot), measure them with `els.js` / `preview.js`, not by eye. Never walk a visible knee by turning a straight leg as one stick; that is allowed only where the knee is hidden (a long skirt, a coat).
- **The cycle, in angles** (thigh from the vertical, plus is forward; knee is the flexion of the shin back; a cartoon exaggerates these by about a third). Walk, a step of 14–20 frames: thigh −20…+25°; support leg - knee 0–5° at the heel strike, 10–15° while the weight comes on, straight again at the push-off; swing leg - knee flexes to 55–70° just after the toe leaves the floor and while the leg passes under the body (the foot is lifted, the boot clears the ground), then extends to 0–5° as the thigh reaches forward. Run, a step of 8–12 frames: thigh −35…+45°; swing knee 90–110° (the heel comes up toward the seat), support knee 20–30° on landing, there is a flight phase when both feet are off the ground. The knee peaks about a quarter of the step after the thigh passes under the body (`lag`), never at the same frame as the hip.
- **Direction: forward is where the face looks.** Write `Facing: left|right` in the plan (the direction the head, the chest and the toes point) and `"facing"` in the scene. Two checks follow from it, both on the frames of the loop: (1) the knee points forward and the shin goes BEHIND the thigh, never ahead of it - for a figure facing left the foot of a bent shin goes to the right of its knee, so the knee bend track is negative (clockwise is plus), for a figure facing right it is positive; `angles.js` prints `KNEE REVERSED` when the sign is wrong, and a reversed knee is a failed scene however big the angle is; (2) the phase: the foot on the ground moves BACK against the body (away from where the face looks) while the body goes forward, the leg in the swing moves FORWARD, and it is the swing leg that bends to 55–70° - a bend on the leg that goes back is the wrong phase and reads as walking backwards on broken legs. In a loop that swaps the legs, the leg whose thigh turns toward the face is the swing one: put the large knee bend there.
- **The body is in time with the legs.** Walking: the body is lowest at the heel strike (both legs apart) and highest when the legs pass, a bob of 6–14 px twice per stride; running: lowest on the support, highest in flight, a bob of 12–25 px. The head follows 2–3 frames later with a half of the amplitude; the arms swing against the legs (the arm opposite to the forward leg goes forward), the elbow bends more on the run (60–90°) than on the walk (10–25°); the torso leans forward 3–6° on a walk and 8–15° on a run.
- **The far leg is wholly under the body and the near leg**, with its boot: the boot is part of the leg and sits in the leg's layer, it does not jump to the front when the leg swings. Check the layer order at every key and in the middle of a swing, not only at rest ("Layer order of limbs" above gives the side).
- **What is drawn on a part moves with the part.** A shadow, a fold, a stripe, a highlight, a sock or features painted on a leg or an arm are cut off with it, or masked to its outline (`maskBy`), so that nothing stays hanging in the air or on the neighbouring part when the limb turns; check the frame of the widest swing. A shadow under the character on the floor is a separate part and stays on the floor.
- **A hand never goes under the clothes it holds in front of.** A hand or a fist with an object (a pot held at the belt, a bag) stays above the body and the skirt on every frame of the loop; the line of the sleeve or the cuff that runs to the hand is part of the hand's chain and turns with it. Check the occlusion on the strongest frames of the swing (`poses.js --frames … --box …`), where the fist comes closest to the skirt.
- **A scene the person already praised is not deepened.** When an approved version exists, a new rule or a judge's remark never widens its swing, deepens its squat, changes the hands' phase or the timing: only the remarks the person made are fixed, everything else stays as it was (a deeper squat and hands moved in phase made a praised scene worse).
